GLP-1
 stands for 
Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 agonists
. This is a class of medications that mimics a hormone naturally produced in the body to help control blood sugar and regulate digestion. Research indicates that GLP-1 receptors are located throughout various systems, including the digestive tract, pancreas, liver, heart, and the central nervous system.
These medications generally assist with weight management through several biological mechanisms.
1 Insulin Regulation
: They stimulate the pancreas to release insulin when blood sugar levels are high and help reduce the amount of sugar the liver releases into the bloodstream.
2 Gastric Emptying
: They slow the rate at which the stomach empties, which helps individuals feel full more quickly and for a longer duration after eating.
3 Appetite Signaling
: They interact with the hypothalamus in the brain to signal satiety. This can help reduce persistent cravings or "food noise," making it easier to focus on balanced nutritional choices.
Understanding Weight Management
Modern medicine views obesity as a complex, chronic condition influenced by genetics, hormones, and neurobiology rather than just willpower. Healthcare providers use metrics like Body Mass Index (BMI) to help categorize weight and monitor health progress. Generally, a BMI over 25 is considered overweight, and a BMI over 30 is categorized as obesity.
Common GLP-1 Medications
Several FDA-approved medications fall into this category, including:
• Liraglutide
 (e.g., Victoza, Saxenda)
• Semaglutide
 (e.g., Ozempic, Wegovy)
• Tirzepatide
 (e.g., Mounjaro, Zepbound) – This specific medication is a dual agonist, targeting both GLP-1 and GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) receptors.
Lifestyle and Nutrition
When using GLP-1 medications, maintaining a nutrient-dense diet is important because appetite is often reduced.
• Protein Intake
: Consuming adequate protein is often recommended to help preserve lean muscle mass during weight loss. Sources include lean meats, fish, beans, tofu, and dairy. I recommend 70-100 grams/day.
• Hydration
: Staying well-hydrated is essential, and some individuals find electrolyte-balanced water helpful.
• Physical Activity
: Incorporating regular physical activity, including resistance training, supports overall health and metabolic function. Resistance training helps prevent loss of lean muscle mass which is inevitable anytime anyone loses weight.
Safety and Side Effects
GLP-1 medications have been used for nearly 20 years, originally developed for the treatment of Type 2 diabetes. While generally considered safe under medical supervision, they are not suitable for everyone.
Contraindications:
These medications are typically not recommended for individuals with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2). They are also generally avoided in patients with a history of pancreatitis or those who are pregnant or breastfeeding.
Common Side Effects:
Side effects are most common when starting the medication or increasing a dose. They may include:
• Nausea
• Diarrhea or Constipation
• Heartburn
• Fatigue
Most side effects are mild and tend to improve as the body adjusts to the medication. Patients are encouraged to consult their healthcare provider if side effects persist or become severe.

Chronic Migraine Prevention with Botox®
Chronic migraines can be debilitating, impacting your work, relationships, and quality of life. Botox® (onabotulinumtoxinA) is an FDA-approved preventive treatment for adults living with chronic migraine, offering a clinically proven approach to reducing both the frequency and severity of migraine attacks before they begin.
Unlike treatments that address symptoms after a migraine starts, Botox® works proactively by interrupting the pain pathways involved in chronic migraine, helping patients experience fewer migraine days and improved daily function.
Who Is a Candidate?
Botox® for chronic migraine is intended for adults diagnosed with chronic migraine, rather than occasional headaches or episodic migraines. You may be a candidate if you:
Experience 15 or more headache days each month
Have at least 8 days per month that meet the criteria for migraine
Have headaches lasting 4 hours or longer
Have not achieved adequate relief with, or have been unable to tolerate, at least two preventive migraine medications
During your consultation, our board-certified Physician Assistant will perform a comprehensive evaluation of your migraine history, symptoms, and previous treatments to determine whether Botox® is the right option for you.
How Botox® Works
Botox® does far more than relax muscles. It works by targeting the sensory nerve endings involved in chronic migraine, helping prevent the release of key pain-signaling neurotransmitters, including calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)and Substance P. By interrupting these pain pathways before they activate, Botox® helps reduce the cascade of inflammation and nerve signaling that contributes to chronic migraine.
